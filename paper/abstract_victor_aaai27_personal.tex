\begin{abstract}
Research knowledge is fragmented across disconnected tools---papers, notes, experiments---with no record of claim origin or confidence, allowing contradictions to accumulate unseen. We present Victor, a local-first research intelligence system that structures knowledge as typed Epistemic Artifacts with mandatory provenance tracking across a five-tier evidence hierarchy (measurement, experiment, literature, note, hypothesis). Victor introduces an NLI-primary conflict-detection architecture that inverts the standard LLM-primary pipeline: a cross-encoder NLI model decides entailment direction while the LLM supplies only reasoning traces, mitigating structured-output agreement bias where instruction-tuned models default to confirmation under uncertainty. A masked-memory primitive suppresses node content from LLM answers while preserving graph structure for routing, with corresponding vector embeddings deleted to close the membership-inference covert channel. The system operates through a bounded Retrieve--Reason--Review--Respond agent loop with hard tool-call limits and automatic pre-answer conflict review. On the SciFact claim verification benchmark, Victor's cross-check configuration (Qwen2.5-7B with DeBERTa-v3-base NLI) achieves 71.0\% accuracy, a 12-point improvement over the pure-LLM baseline. Victor is CPU-only and requires no cloud connectivity, enabling research intelligence in disconnected environments.
\end{abstract}
